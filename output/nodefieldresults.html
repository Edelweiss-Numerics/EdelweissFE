Node field results

Every node field (NodeField) holds the same five entries, from its construction on: U, dU, P, V and A (ENTRIES). There are no others, and no code creates one. These are the names a field output asks for with result=:

*fieldOutput
>>perNode, nSet=bottom, field=displacement, result=P, name=RF

Each entry has one meaning, the same in every solver. This page defines it.

Who owns what

One rule decides where a value lives: the model owns the state the next attempt starts from; a solver owns only what lives within one discrete system. An entry is therefore of one of two kinds:

  • state – what a solver reads back from the model whenever it (re)builds its discrete system, and what a restart checkpoint must carry for the run to continue;

  • result – what a solver writes for output and for the user; no solver reads it back.

Iterations work on the solver’s vectors only. A cut back increment writes nothing. An accepted increment writes every entry, once (the explicit meshfree solver in two parts, see below). The exceptions are the increments of the dynamic solvers NED and NID that integrate nothing: they evaluate no force, so they leave P as the last accepted increment wrote it, of this step or of the step before. These are

  • the zero-length increment before the first increment of a step, in both; NED’s leaves the acceleration A as well;

  • in NID, also an increment of negligible length: the round-off remainder the time stepper may leave at the end of a step (see nonlinearimplicitdynamic).

A discrete system with a new numbering (a changed mesh, a changed active domain, a changed set of scalar variables) reads the state entries from the model; new locations on the same numbering (a changed connectivity, e.g. a contact search) do not: the vectors of the solver keep their values, which are the state. A refinement of the mesh interpolates the state entries onto the nodes it creates; the results there are zero until the next accepted increment.

Every solver passes these entries through the discrete system (DiscreteSystem), and in no other way: readStateFromModel() when it (re)builds the system, writeResultsToModel() when it accepts an increment, and, if it integrates in time, writeVelocityAndAccelerationToModel() for the velocity and the acceleration. A quasi-static solver does not integrate in time: the state it accepts is at rest, and it writes zero to V and A of every node field (writeZeroVelocityAndAccelerationToModel()), so that a dynamic step after it starts from rest.

A step hands the next one the model’s state at its end time: the solution, and the velocity and the acceleration at that time. The explicit solvers integrate the velocity staggered half an increment behind the solution, and so write that staggered velocity at every accepted increment; at the end of their step, the model advances it by the half increment it lags behind, with the acceleration (advanceVelocity(), the one change of these entries that needs no discrete system). A dynamic step starts from this state: the implicit dynamic solver from the velocity, completing the acceleration from equilibrium; the explicit solver NED from both, without evaluating anything (see nonlinearexplicitdynamic).

In the explicit solver (NED), the inertial and damping forces of a node with an equation of motion are the net force the next velocity update divides by, so its P is the internal force plus that net force: the force of the loads and the constraints acting there. At a node with a prescribed motion, the acceleration at the end of an increment is set by the prescribed velocity of the next one, which is not known when the increment is accepted, so its P is the internal force alone: the support reaction, without the inertia of the support node’s own lumped mass, and its A is zero. A multi-point-constraint slave integrates no equation of motion of its own: its inertia and its force are folded onto its masters, so its P is its internal force, and it takes its V and A from its masters. Elsewhere, its A is the acceleration with which the velocity at the end of the increment, half an increment after its V, satisfies the equation of motion. The net force itself is the solver’s state, not an entry (see nonlinearexplicitdynamic).

Models whose discrete system covers a part of their nodes

A meshfree model (EdelweissMeshfree) has nodes that carry no total solution: the nodes of the background grid of the material point method and the nodes of the kernel functions of RKPM are incremental – their degrees of freedom are the coefficients of the increment, starting at zero in every increment, while the particles and material points carry the state. On these nodes, U is zero and dU holds the coefficients of the last accepted increment, which is their meaningful nodal value. Nodes outside the active domain of an increment hold zero in every entry.

The explicit meshfree solver carries the velocity of the kernel functions in V: it writes V by node at acceptance and reads it back by node whenever its discrete system gets a new numbering, also at the start of a step, so that a node keeps its velocity when the active domain changes around it; a node that becomes active starts at rest. Its increment ends on other kernel functions than it started on – the particles move, and reach other nodes – so it writes the results in two parts, each through the discrete system that holds them: dU on the nodes the increment was taken on, P, V and A on the nodes of the configuration it ends in. A node that left the active domain in that increment therefore keeps its dU, and holds zero in P, V and A. Its zero-length increment evaluates the forces of the configuration the step starts from, and writes them, when the model’s nodes are at rest; a step that continues the motion the model holds at its nodes evaluates no force there, and leaves P as it is.

The implicit dynamic solver (NID) integrates the inertia of the entities that integrate their own either in the entities or at the nodes they couple (option inertia, see nonlinearimplicitdynamic). In the entities, they carry their velocity and acceleration in their state, and V and A of the nodes they couple are zero; at the nodes, NID carries them in V and A by node. Either way, P holds the inertial force: in the entities’ residual contributions, or added at the nodes. The two keep the motion of the entities in different places, and nothing transfers it between them: a step whose inertia would take it from where the model does not hold it refuses to start (carriersOfTheMotion()).

Using the entries in code

The entries exist from the construction of a node field on, also in a model built programmatically; a resize (for instance, by adaptive mesh refinement) keeps all of them. Reading or writing an entry through the DofNumbering refuses a name that is not one of the five, and a node field the DofNumbering does not number: a node field is read or written as a whole only by the DofNumbering that numbers it, since its rows are addressed by position; on a node set, its rows are found by node, so that any node field of a numbered field can be read or written there (e.g. the node field of the model, while the discrete system numbers that of an active domain). A restart checkpoint holds every entry of every node field, and a checkpoint lacking one is refused (see Restart).
