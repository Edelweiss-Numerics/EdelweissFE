The residual and the tangent

Every entity that enters the discrete system – an element, a load, a constraint – contributes two things: a vector \(\boldsymbol{P}\) to the residual, and its tangent \(\boldsymbol{K} = \partial \boldsymbol{P} / \partial \boldsymbol{U}\). The rule is the same for all of them:

  • An element adds its internal forces, \(\boldsymbol{P} = \boldsymbol{f}_\mathrm{int} = \int_\Omega \boldsymbol{B}^T \boldsymbol{\sigma} \, \mathrm{d}V\), and its stiffness matrix.

  • A load adds the negative of the external load it applies: a node force \(\boldsymbol{F}\) adds \(-\boldsymbol{F}\), a body force \(-\int_\Omega \boldsymbol{N}^T \boldsymbol{b} \, \mathrm{d}V\). A dead load has no tangent; a load that follows the deformation, such as a pressure on the current surface, has one.

  • A constraint adds the negative of the force it exerts on its nodes – just what an element of the same mechanics would add as its internal force. A penalty spring of stiffness \(k\) that pulls a degree of freedom \(u\) towards \(\bar{u}\) adds \(P = k\,(u - \bar{u})\) and \(K = k\); a Lagrange multiplier \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\) enforcing \(\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{u}) = \boldsymbol{0}\) adds \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^T \partial \boldsymbol{g} / \partial \boldsymbol{u}\) to the rows of the nodes and \(\boldsymbol{g}\) to the rows of the multipliers. See Constraints for writing one.

A solver sums the contributions in three loops of every iteration: computeElements over the elements, assembleLoads over the loads – every load assembles its own contribution, wherever it acts (see LoadBase) – and assembleConstraints over the constraints of the discrete system (see Constraints). Summed over all entities,

\[\boldsymbol{G}(\boldsymbol{U}) = \sum \boldsymbol{P} = \boldsymbol{f}_\mathrm{int} - \boldsymbol{f}_\mathrm{ext}\]

is the out-of-balance force, which equilibrium makes zero, and the summed \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is its derivative. Newton’s method replaces \(\boldsymbol{G}(\boldsymbol{U} + \Delta \boldsymbol{U}) = \boldsymbol{0}\) by its linearization \(\boldsymbol{G} + \boldsymbol{K}\, \Delta \boldsymbol{U} = \boldsymbol{0}\), so the implicit solvers solve

\[\boldsymbol{K}\, \Delta \boldsymbol{U} = \boldsymbol{R}, \qquad \boldsymbol{R} = -\boldsymbol{G} = \boldsymbol{f}_\mathrm{ext} - \boldsymbol{f}_\mathrm{int}\]

in every iteration. The right-hand side \(\boldsymbol{R}\) is what the convergence criteria check, and what the multi-point-constraint condensation and the Dirichlet boundary conditions act on. The other solvers use the same sums:

  • NID subtracts the inertia and damping forces from \(\boldsymbol{R}\), and adds their tangents to \(\boldsymbol{K}\).

  • NED accelerates the nodes with the net force \(\boldsymbol{R}\), and needs no tangent.

  • NISTPArcLength sums the loads twice, at the start of the step (the dead load, \(\boldsymbol{P}_0\)) and at its end; their difference is the reference load, \(\boldsymbol{P}_\mathrm{ref}\). Scaled by the load parameter \(\lambda\), it enters \(\boldsymbol{R} = -(\boldsymbol{P}_0 + \lambda \boldsymbol{P}_\mathrm{ref} + \boldsymbol{P}_\mathrm{elements} + \boldsymbol{P}_\mathrm{constraints})\).

Why one rule? Because then \(\boldsymbol{K}\) is always the derivative of exactly what was added to \(\boldsymbol{P}\), for every entity alike. That can be checked by finite differences, entity by entity, and the same check catches a contribution whose sign alone is wrong: \(\boldsymbol{K}\) then comes out as \(-\partial \boldsymbol{P} / \partial \boldsymbol{U}\). A contribution negated together with its tangent passes it, so tests/test_residual_tangent_convention.py checks every constraint this way and also checks the direction of the forces it exerts (see Constraints); a dead load has no tangent, so there the sign of each element load is checked against the load integrated independently.

The elements’ sum alone, \(\boldsymbol{f}_\mathrm{int}\), is what the quasi-static solvers report as the nodal result P of a field output, and the dynamic solvers add the inertial and damping forces to it (Node field results): at a degree of freedom with a Dirichlet boundary condition and no load, it is the reaction force. NED’s net force \(\boldsymbol{R}\) is its own state, and is not reported.
