beam - Beam elements

A section for beam elements: the Marmot Euler-Bernoulli beams B2D2, B2D3, B3D2, B3D3 and their co-rotational (finite rotation) variants B2D2CR and B3D2CR. They integrate their cross section over section points (fibers), each with its own material instance (any Marmot hypoelastic material). This section generates the fibers (see edelweissfe.utils.beamsections) and passes them, with the orientation and the torsion constant, as element properties. The element documentation is Marmot’s beam element page.

Profiles (profile=):

  • rectangle (b along z, h along y; nY x nZ fibers),

  • circle (d) and tube (d, di; nRings x nSectors fibers),

  • iprofile (h, b, tf, tw; nY fibers along the web, nZ across a flange, nT through a flange or the web thickness),

  • polygon (polygonFile: vertices y, z per line, loops separated by empty lines, holes by the even-odd rule; meshSize, triangleOrder),

  • points (pointsFile: y, z, area per line, or y, area),

  • generic (area, Iz, 3D: Iy; 2 or 4 fibers reproducing them).

The 1D rule of the parametric profiles is rule=gauss|lobatto|simpson (lobatto and simpson place fibers on the outer edges, which detects the first yield exactly). The torsion constant J (3D) is computed for rectangles (Roark), circles and tubes (exact) and I-profiles (thin-walled, \(\sum b t^3 / 3\)); it is required for the other profiles. With centroidAtAxis=True (default) the fibers are shifted such that their centroid lies on the beam axis (the line through the nodes); otherwise the given coordinates are measured from the axis (an eccentric beam).

Local axes: the local y axis lies in the plane of a 2D beam; in 3D it is the part of the orientation vector (vx, vy, vz) (Marmot’s \(\mathbf{v}\)) normal to the beam axis, and \(z = x \times y\). In 3D the orientation vector is required, except for circles and tubes. In 2D, the z coordinates of the fibers are dropped (use nZ=1 for rectangles).

multiplicity=k makes the beam stand for k identical parallel members (the fiber areas, A, I and J are scaled by k, the coordinates are not), e.g., k fibers stacked through the thickness of a 2D model, as the area of a truss.

One material per element: a composite section (e.g., steel and concrete) is modeled by several beam elements on the same nodes, one per material, each with its part of the section (the fiber integration is additive). The parts must measure their coordinates from one common axis: give them centroidAtAxis=False (enforced). The parametric profiles (rectangle, iprofile, tube, circle) are generated about the origin, so an off-axis part needs a pointsFile or polygonFile with its coordinates from the common axis. The torsion constants of the parts are added, which is not the torsion constant of a composite (in particular a closed) section; give J per part accordingly. An eccentric section (a non-concentric composite, or centroidAtAxis=False) couples axial strain and bending: the 3-node beams represent it exactly for a tip load, the 2-node beams (axial strain constant per element) converge with \(h^2\).

The co-rotational variants take the same section. For B3D2CR the rotation dofs are total rotation vectors: a nodal moment load on the rotation field acts on these dofs and is a physical moment only for small rotations (apply finite-rotation moments as force couples).

Units: none of their own, use one consistent system, e.g., N, mm, MPa.

Section points (module edelweissfe.utils.beamsections):

Integration points of beam cross sections (“section points”, the fibers of Marmot’s BeamFiberSection), for beam elements that integrate their section over given points, e.g., the Marmot beams B<d>D<n>. The triangle rules of the polygons come from edelweissfe.utils.trianglequadrature.

A section is a set of points \((y_i, z_i)\) with areas (weights) \(A_i\) in the section coordinates \((y, z)\) of the beam. The generators cover

  • parametric shapes with a selectable 1D rule and number of points per direction: rectangle (\(n_y \times n_z\)), circle and tube (rings \(\times\) sectors), iProfile (per flange and web),

  • polygons with holes (even-odd rule), decomposed exactly into trapezoids, subdivided to a mesh size and integrated with a triangle rule of selectable order,

  • explicit point lists.

The 1D rules (on \([-1, 1]\)): gauss (Gauss-Legendre, \(n \geq 1\)), lobatto (Gauss-Lobatto, \(n \geq 2\), points on the outer fibers) and simpson (composite Simpson, odd \(n \geq 3\), points on the outer fibers). With points on the outer fibers, the first yield of a section is detected exactly. The second moments of area are integrated exactly by gauss with \(n \geq 2\), lobatto with \(n \geq 3\) and simpson.

edelweissfe.utils.beamsections.polygon(loops, meshSize=None, order=2)[source]

A polygonal section, optionally with holes.

The polygon is decomposed exactly into trapezoids (between the y levels of its vertices), which are subdivided to the meshSize, split into triangles and integrated with a triangle rule of the polynomial degree order (1, 2, 4 or 5; 2 or more integrates the second moments of area exactly).

Parameters:

  • meshSize (float) – The largest size of the cells; default: a tenth of the larger extent of the section.

  • order (int) – The polynomial degree of the triangle rule.

truss - Truss (bar) elements

A section for truss (bar) elements, the Marmot truss elements, assigning the cross section area (in the units of the model, e.g., mm² for N, mm, MPa). Element and material go in pairs:

element

materials

T2D2, T2D3, T3D2, T3D3

hypoelastic (geometrically linear), e.g., LinearElastic, VonMises

T2D2CR, T2D3CR, T3D2CR, T3D3CR

finite strain (rigid rotations stress free), e.g., CompressibleNeoHooke, FiniteStrainJ2Plasticity, VonMises/Hughes-Winget

A material of the other kind is rejected by Marmot with a hint to the matching element. Use the CR elements whenever the bars rotate, as the geometrically linear ones strain under a finite rotation. A truss has no lateral stiffness: restrain nodes only connected to collinear bars, and the transverse modes of the mid node of quadratic bars, unless the bars are embedded (see Marmot’s truss element page).

Results of the quadrature points: normal force, stress (axial Cauchy stress), strain, elastic energy, dissipation, the material states, and for the CR elements lateral stretches and kirchhoff stress. A complete worked example with the hand check \(N = E A \Delta / L\) is tests/test_trusses.py::test_truss_small_strain_inclined.
