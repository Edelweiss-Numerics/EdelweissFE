Composed linear solvers: reference

The arguments, defaults and data lines of the components and of the adapter, for looking up; how to build a solver from them is on Composing a linear solver, the method they implement on Theory: the field-split AMG preconditioner, and the classes and functions on Linear solvers: Python API.

The adapter

ComposedLinearSolver is what the nonlinear solver calls, x = solver(A, b), once per Newton iteration, for every type of linear solver. It finds what changed since the last call (the first solve, a new model, a resized system, a new sparsity pattern, a jump of the right-hand side that marks a new increment), hands the matrix down the tree, applies the root (to each column of b, if it has several), and reports on the solve. Its settings are the data lines every type has (the table in Linear solvers):

*linearSolver, name=mySolver, type=custom
script=solver.py
** silent, warning (default), info, debug
verbosity=info
** a solve with more outer iterations gets a warning (default 100)
warningOuterIterations=60
** a right-hand side this many times the last one is a new increment (default 4)
residualGrowthFactor=4.0
** none (default), slow, every, or the numbers of the solves: 12 13 14
dumps=slow
dumpDirectory=slowSolves
maxDumps=10
dumpContextSolves=2
** custom only: run the script again whenever it changes, during the run
hotReload=true

In Python, the same adapter is ComposedLinearSolver(tree, verbosity=..., warningOuterIterations=..., residualGrowthFactor=..., dumps=SolveDumps(directory, trigger, maxDumps=..., contextSolves=...)), or ComposedLinearSolver.fromScript("solver.py", hotReload=True, ...); nodeCoordinates and midsideCorners give node coordinates and midside corners per field instead of those of the model (an offline replay without a model).

Verbosity levels

Messages go through the shared Journal, and the data line verbosity decides whether the solver attempts to log at all – the Journal’s own level then decides whether the attempt is shown. All messages are nested inside the Newton iteration they belong to; warnings are marked ⚠.

silent

Nothing at all.

warning (default)

Only abnormal conditions, so a healthy run stays quiet: an outer iteration count past warningOuterIterations (a slow solve), a true residual still above the requested tolerance after every true-residual restart (saying whether an outer pass stopped at the iteration cap, or whether, on a scaled system, the scaled residual was met but not the true one), and any dump written. A pass that stopped at the cap but whose solve still met the tolerance, after a true-residual restart, is not reported; the hierarchies are rebuilt all the same.

info

Turns on the “linear solve” column of the nonlinear solver’s per-iteration table: outer iteration count, achieved true residual (and whether it met the requested tolerance) and number of true-residual restarts of each solve; and shows the structure of the solver, in a frame, at its first solve. The frame stands above the header of the table, so that nothing the solver says interrupts the rows. This is the level to run at when first characterising a model on an iterative solver.

debug

Adds, per solve, whether the hierarchies were rebuilt (and why, e.g. rebuilt(stale)) or reused, the requested tolerance and the wall-clock, in the “linear solve” column, and one line per true-residual restart of a Krylov solve on a scaled system. After a rebuild, the notes of the components on what they built follow in the column: for a SchurComplement, the condensed dofs and the nonzeros of \(\tilde S\).

The sizes of what a SchurComplement built are also recorded in its statistics (condensed dofs, S̃ nnz), see the run-statistics view.

Per-stage wall-clock – equilibration, the equilibrated system (the scaled operator and the coupling blocks between fields, built in one threaded pass), hierarchy build (per field, and per stage of a p-multigrid build), near-null-space construction, outer solve, and true-residual restarts – is recorded through performancetiming regardless of verbosity, and appears nested under “linear solve” in the job’s performance table. That table, not the log, is the first place to look when deciding whether a run is dominated by hierarchy builds or by outer iterations.

Dumps of the solves

The data line dumps (in Python SolveDumps(directory, trigger, maxDumps, contextSolves)) writes the raw systems of chosen solves to dumpDirectory, for an offline replay (scripts/benchmark_linsolve.py, which replays a dumped sequence through direct solver variants, or a ComposedLinearSolver driven by hand with setFieldStructure and the dumped geometry as nodeCoordinates and midsideCorners). It works for every type, direct solvers included, and is the one way to capture a solve. A dump is the raw (A, b) of a solve – before any diagonal scaling – with the field-block layout active for that solve, which is what a replay needs in order to go back through the same tree without a live model: a sequence of authentic systems, so that every solver variant replayed offline sees the same input. The trigger:

  • slow: a solve with more outer iterations than warningOuterIterations (so that a dump and its warning fire together), or one stopped at the iteration cap short of its tolerance. A preconditioner that degrades typically shows only tens of hours into a run, at a solve nobody can name in advance (a late-increment, heavily damaged Jacobian, say); unlike every or solve numbers, fixed before the run, this trigger decides by the solve’s own outcome;

  • every: every solve, a sequence (whether a factorization or a preconditioner can be reused is a question about a sequence, not a single matrix);

  • solve numbers, e.g. dumps=12 13 14: these solves of each linear solver (counted from 1).

On disk:

  • A_<solver>_<solve>.npz (SciPy, uncompressed), b_<solver>_<solve>.npy, and with a Schur complement the condensed dofs, schurDofs_<solver>_<solve>.npy; <solver> is the ordinal of the linear solver in the process (one per nonlinear solver, made anew only when a step names another linear solver), so the dumps of several solvers never overwrite each other, and the solve numbering continues across steps and a hot reload;

  • geometry_<solver>_<solve>.npz: what a replay of the tree needs of the model, the node coordinates of every vector field (<field>_coordinates, for its rigid-body modes) and the midside corners the tree used (<field>_isCorner, <field>_edgeEndpoints);

  • one line per dumped system in manifest.jsonl: solveCount, role ("trigger" or "context"), triggerSolveCount, contextOffset, rows, nnz, matrixFile, rhsFile, geometryFile, the field blocks (name, start, stop, dimension), patternChanged, newIncrement, and for a Krylov solve its SolveRecord – iterations, residual (the true relative residual), eta, capHit, trueResidualRestarts, rebuildReason (null: the preconditioner was reused), scaledSystem – and previousIterations.

dumpContextSolves also writes the solves before a dumped one, kept in memory as copies. A single snapshot cannot distinguish “this operator is intrinsically hard” from “this solve’s own carried-over state degraded independently of the operator” – such as a reused, now-stale hierarchy. Both look identical from the matrix alone, and a fresh solver replaying the matrix on its own can converge in a fraction of the live iteration count, which is a genuinely misleading result. Capturing the preceding sequence lets an offline replay feed one persistent solver instance the same solves in the same order. The staleness question can often be answered from the manifest alone, without a replay. maxDumps (default 10) bounds the systems written to one directory in the process, by all solvers that dump there, context solves included. A sequence of every solve is dumps=every (or solve numbers).

Hot reload of the solver script

With hotReload=true (type=custom), the adapter reads the script before every solve and, if it changed, runs it again and continues with the tree it builds, set up on that solve (rebuild reason reload). The settings of the adapter stay those of the input file, and what the run has learnt stays too: the solve count, the residual history, the numbering of the dumps. The journal says so at warning, with the sha256 of the script and the new tree, so that a reader of the log knows which solver was in force from which solve onwards. This makes solver settings testable on a live run, at the state that was expensive to reach.

A bad edit can never end the run: a script that cannot be read or does not compile (e.g. half written by an editor) is retried on the next solve; one that raises, or defines no composed solver, is reported (REJECTED) and the tree in force kept until the script changes again; a new tree whose set-up fails (e.g. an AMGCL tree naming an unknown smoother) is ROLLED BACK to the one in force. The contents themselves are the stamp, not the modification time, which coarse file timestamps can leave unchanged across two quick edits.

Reuse and tolerance

ComposedLinearSolver finds what changed since the last solve (the first solve, a new model, a resized system, a new sparsity pattern, a jump of the right-hand side that marks a new increment) and hands it down the tree as a MatrixChange. LGMRES adds its own reasons – a new increment, a stale preconditioner (too many more iterations than the solve it was built for, or a cap hit), or reusePreconditioner=False – and every component decides in update what to keep: an AMG hierarchy is kept unless a rebuild is asked for, a DiagonalScaling keeps its \(D\), a Multiplicative always takes the new couplings.

A DiagonalScaling rebuilds its \(D\) together with what is below it, also for the reasons a Krylov solver below it adds itself (Preconditioner.withRebuildReasons), so that DiagonalScaling(LGMRES(...)) rebuilds as LGMRES(DiagonalScaling(...)) does.

What is rebuilt is first forgotten (Preconditioner.forget): a Krylov solver that rebuilds its preconditioner drops all of it before any part is built again, a SchurComplement drops \(\tilde S\) and its child before it forms the next \(\tilde S\), the blocks of the last matrix before it cuts out those of this one, and a direct solve its factorization before it factorizes again; the adapter forgets the whole tree when the model changes (a new step, a change of the mesh). So the old and the new hierarchies, factorizations and matrices are never held at once. What is reused on purpose survives: the hierarchies while no rebuild is asked for, the selection of the condensed dofs, a symbolic factorization (PARDISO’s reuseSymbolicFactorization), and what the Krylov solver has learnt (its reference iterations, the gap of a ResidualGapCompensation, its scratch vectors).

Performance: a diagonal scaling does not form \(D A D\); the first component below it that needs a block forms \(\hat A\) and all field blocks in one threaded native pass, kept with the matrix for all others, which also keeps \(A\) as the operator of the outer Krylov solve (SystemMatrix).

A Krylov solve on the scaled system

Where the diagonal scaling sits says what the Krylov solve converges. LGMRES(DiagonalScaling(M)) (the default of blockAMG()) solves \(A x = b\), preconditioned by \(D \hat M^{-1} D\): its residual is the true one, \(\lVert b - A x \rVert / \lVert b \rVert\). DiagonalScaling(LGMRES(M)) (blockAMG(krylovOnScaledSystem=True)) solves \(\hat A \hat x = D b\) with \(\hat A = D A D\), \(x = D \hat x\), and converges the scaled residual \(\lVert D (b - A x) \rVert\), which weighs every row by its own diagonal – a field with small residual entries is resolved as well as the others – but which can be smaller than the true one, on which the solve is accepted.

So a Krylov solver on a scaled matrix checks the true residual after its pass (in the physical variables of its problem: \(b - A x = D^{-1} (\hat b - \hat A \hat x)\)) and, while it misses \(\eta\), restarts from its iterate with a tighter tolerance, 0.01 times the last, at most trueResidualRestarts times (default 2). With residualGapCompensation=ResidualGapCompensation(safetyFactor=0.3, maxFactor=1e3) the first pass asks for \(s \, \eta / g\) instead, with \(g\) the ratio of the true to the scaled residual learnt from the previous solves, and a true-residual restart tightens by the measured ratio; it is opt-in (Gap compensation). On an unscaled matrix there are no true-residual restarts: a pass that misses \(\eta\) has hit the iteration cap, which a warm restart would only extend. The method is True-residual enforcement: LGMRES on the scaled system.

A preconditioner on the filtered matrix

AMG(dropTolerance=τ) and TwoLevel(..., dropTolerance=τ) build on the filtered block \(\tilde A\): the off-diagonal entries with \(\lvert a_{ij} \rvert < \tau \sqrt{\lvert a_{ii} a_{jj} \rvert}\) dropped, the diagonal always kept. For operators with many entries per row that are numerically far sparser than structurally (e.g. RKPM), smoothed aggregation then weighs only the entries that matter, and the Galerkin products carry fewer of them to the coarse levels. Only the preconditioner sees \(\tilde A\); the Krylov solve applies \(A\), so the solution is that of \(A\). lumpDroppedEntries=True adds the dropped entries of each row onto its diagonal, which keeps the row sums (the “filtered matrix” of the AMG literature); for elasticity, whose off-diagonals are mostly negative, this weakens the diagonal dominance. The run statistics record the stored entries before and after (nnz, nnz kept). blockAMG(dropTolerance=τ, lumpDroppedEntries=...) passes them to every field.

The AMGCL backend of a field

AMG and TwoLevel take the AMGCL tree and the backend apart (the method: Per-field AMG hierarchies: AMG): the tree is what AMGCL reads, the backend how EdelweissFE stores the field’s matrix for it. The defaults are chosen in one place, backendOf(), from the field:

Keyword

Default

backendBlockSize

the field’s dimension for a 2D or 3D field (node-wise blocks: less index and value traffic per product), else 1 (scalar entries)

backendPrecision

"float" on 3x3 blocks (set up in double, only the matrices the cycle reads stored in float), else "double"

nearNullspace (AMG only)

"rigidBodyModes": the field’s rigid-body modes as far as the backend holds them – on a block backend the translations \(D^{-1} t\) of the scaled block (instead of AMGCL’s constant blocks), on the scalar backend the translations and rotations of a displacement field, the translations of another vector field. "translations" takes the translations on either backend, None AMGCL’s own near null-space

So AMG(backendBlockSize=1) puts a 3D displacement field on the scalar backend, in double, where its hierarchy takes all rigid-body modes as its near null-space. Rotations belong to the near null-space of a displacement field only (by its phenomenon, carriesRigidBodyModes()): the rotation field of a micropolar model gets its translations.

Default field trees

AMG(amgclTree=...) and TwoLevel(..., amgclTree=...) take a field’s AMGCL parameter tree, plus EdelweissFE’s own key spectralRadiusSteps (below). A tree given replaces the default outright, so start from the one that applies and change only what is intended. The defaults are, by the field’s nodal dimension (defaultFieldTree() in the code):

{
    "3D vector field (e.g. displacement)": {
        "coarsening": {
            "type": "smoothed_aggregation",
            "aggr": {"eps_strong": 0.01},
            "estimate_spectral_radius": true
        },
        "relax": {"type": "chebyshev", "degree": 16, "lower": 0.01, "higher": 1.1},
        "npre": 0,
        "npost": 1
    },
    "2D vector field": {
        "coarsening": {
            "type": "smoothed_aggregation",
            "aggr": {"eps_strong": 0.01},
            "estimate_spectral_radius": true
        },
        "relax": {"type": "chebyshev", "degree": 16, "lower": 0.01, "higher": 1.1},
        "npre": 0,
        "npost": 1
    },
    "vector field of a dimension other than 2 or 3": {
        "coarsening": {
            "type": "smoothed_aggregation",
            "aggr": {"eps_strong": 0.01},
            "estimate_spectral_radius": true
        },
        "relax": {"type": "chebyshev", "degree": 16, "lower": 0.01, "higher": 1.1},
        "npre": 0,
        "npost": 1
    },
    "scalar field (e.g. nonlocal damage)": {
        "coarsening": {"type": "smoothed_aggregation"},
        "relax": {"type": "chebyshev"}
    }
}

The backend the tree is built on is chosen apart from it, by the keywords backendBlockSize, backendPrecision and (AMG only) nearNullspace, with defaults from the field (backendOf()): node-wise 3x3 blocks stored in float for a 3D field, 2x2 blocks in double for a 2D one (this wrapper has no float 2x2 backend), the scalar backend in double otherwise, and on a block backend the translations \(D^{-1} t\) as the near null-space. A vector field of a dimension the defaults were never tuned for (anything but 2 or 3, e.g. a 4-DOF-per-node formulation) gets the same coarsening and relaxation as the 2D and 3D fields, on the scalar backend. AMG(backendBlockSize=1) puts a 2D or 3D displacement field on the scalar backend, the only one that takes the rotations (The near-null-space of a block backend); a tree with another coarsening than smoothed aggregation on a block backend needs nearNullspace=None, which is checked.

A linear 3D displacement field of the blockAMG() preset gets the rigid-body coarse level (TwoLevel(coarseSpace=RigidBodyModesPerAggregate())), configured by the same tree: its relax and npre/npost are the fine smoother of the rigid-body coarse level, its coarsening the aggregation of that level, and the whole tree the AMGCL hierarchy below it. Only a genuinely scalar field (nodal dimension 1) gets the plain scalar tree, which predates the vector recipe and was never re-tuned against it. AMGCL warns on stderr about a parameter key it does not recognise and otherwise ignores it, so check its stderr if an override appears to do nothing; a Chebyshev relax, EdelweissFE’s own, rejects such a key instead.

The corner-node two-level cycle’s default tree is another: TwoLevel(coarseSpace=CornerNodeInterpolation()) reads its fine smoother from the tree in the same spelling (relax, npre, npost; it has no coarsening of its own). For the corner-node two-level cycle, degree 16 is stronger than it pays: degree 8 costs half the products per sweep for fewer than twice the outer iterations (The smoother of the corner-node two-level cycle). So a TwoLevel on CornerNodeInterpolation that is given no tree takes CornerNodeInterpolation.defaultTree(field): for a 3D displacement field, the vector field’s tree with a Chebyshev polynomial of degree 8 on the interval from 0.03 to 1.1 times the spectral radius (defaultCornerNodeTwoLevelTree()), where it was measured; any other field keeps its default tree (defaultFieldTree(field.dimension)):

{
    "coarsening": {
        "type": "smoothed_aggregation",
        "aggr": {"eps_strong": 0.01},
        "estimate_spectral_radius": true
    },
    "relax": {"type": "chebyshev", "degree": 8, "lower": 0.03, "higher": 1.1},
    "npre": 0,
    "npost": 1
}

AMG() and TwoLevel(coarseSpace=RigidBodyModesPerAggregate()) alone keep degree 16: there, degree 8 gained nothing. Each level has its own tree. One given to the corner-node two-level cycle (TwoLevel(coarseSpace=CornerNodeInterpolation(), amgclTree=...)) is its fine smoother only; the coarse level has the tree of its coarse solve, CornerNodeInterpolation(coarseSolve=...). The default coarse solve, DiagonalScaling(AMG()), takes AMG’s default tree; the blockAMG() preset gives a 3D displacement field the same smoother on both levels, CornerNodeInterpolation(coarseSolve=DiagonalScaling(TwoLevel(coarseSpace= RigidBodyModesPerAggregate(), amgclTree=defaultCornerNodeTwoLevelTree()))).

Every spectral radius in these trees – of the Chebyshev smoother (relax) and of the prolongation smoothing (coarsening with estimate_spectral_radius) – is estimated by spectralRadiusSteps Arnoldi steps (default 10) from a start vector seeded per row, the same at any thread count (Near-null-space and the Chebyshev smoother). The key is EdelweissFE’s own, read by its Chebyshev smoother and its smoothed aggregation; the trees leave the default. A tree that sets AMGCL’s power_iters, which the Arnoldi steps replace, is refused.

The rigid-body coarse level: RigidBodyModesPerAggregate and TwoLevel

The blockAMG() preset (type=blockamg) gives a linear 3D displacement field the rigid-body coarse level, and the corner-node field of a quadratic one too (as the coarse solve of its corner-node two-level cycle); rigidBodyModesPerAggregate=false switches it off, cornerNodeInterpolation=false the corner-node two-level cycle. A 2D displacement field, or a 3D one with other options, gets it in a custom solver script, OnField(name, TwoLevel(coarseSpace=RigidBodyModesPerAggregate(...), ...)). The field must be on a block backend (backendBlockSize 2 or 3, the default of a 2D or 3D field), and its tree must smooth with a Chebyshev polynomial (relax.type chebyshev, without relax.scale, relax.spectralRadiusSteps at least 1) and coarsen by smoothed aggregation, as the default trees do; TwoLevel checks this when it meets its field, and the parameters of RigidBodyModesPerAggregate at construction. The options of RigidBodyModesPerAggregate:

prolongation ("smoothed")

"smoothed": \(P = (I - \omega\, \mathrm{diag}_B(\hat A_F)^{-1} \hat A_F) P^{\mathrm{tent}}\). "tentative": \(P^{\mathrm{tent}}\) itself – a cheaper setup and a sparser coarse operator, for more outer iterations (32 instead of 22 on the elastic tunnel system).

coarseHierarchyPrecision ("double")

The value type of the AMGCL hierarchy below: "double", or "float" (AMGCL’s single-precision backend, its setup included) for half its memory, at the price of more outer iterations.

The arguments of SchurComplement

The method is Schur complement condensation: SchurComplement (opt-in), a complete script A Schur complement. The arguments of SchurComplement:

condensedDofMarkers (required)

A list of markers, created through the marker registry from the options of a >>marker line of *modelModifier, type=hAdaptivity, e.g. getMarkerClass("fieldOutput").fromOptions({"fieldOutput": "damage", "threshold": 0.05, "halo": 1}). Sets, surfaces and fieldOutputs they name must exist.

schurComplementSolve (required)

The preconditioner of \(\tilde S\), e.g. DiagonalScaling(Multiplicative(...)).

approximation ("local")

"local": \(\tilde S\) as in Schur complement condensation: SchurComplement (opt-in). "none": \(\tilde S = A_{kk}\), the coupling through \(b\) ignored (for comparison).

dropTolerance (1e-4)

\(\tau\), the relative size below which entries of \(\tilde S\) are dropped.

windowNodes, haloNodes (50, windowNodes)

The nodes of a window’s core along a chain, and of the halo on either side. A chain no longer than one core with both halos is corrected exactly.

blockFactors ("upper")

"upper": the block upper triangular factor, one solve with \(A_{bb}\) per application. "both": both factors of the block LU factorization, two solves.

condensedBlockSolve (DirectSolver("superlu"))

The solver of \(A_{bb}\).
